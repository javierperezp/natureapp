How we score food.

A transparent, ingredient-based estimate of food processing—not a health or safety score. It's influenced by the NOVA framework, but it is not an official NOVA assignment.

01   We read the ingredient list

Enter ingredients yourself, take a photo for optical character recognition (OCR), or look up a barcode in Open Food Facts. We don't assume what's in the product based on its brand or marketing.

02   We look for processing clues

Industrial formulations, flavourings, emulsifiers, sweeteners, colourings, protein isolates and modified starches are given the greatest weight. Added sugar, salt, refined flour and longer lists contribute smaller deductions. Ordinary ingredients like oil and salt are not automatically treated as ultra-processed.

03   We estimate a score from 0–100

We start at 100, show deductions by ingredient category, and apply a processing-category ceiling when the ingredients clearly indicate added culinary ingredients or an industrial formulation. The score is a custom heuristic and has not been clinically validated.

85–100 · Minimally processedPrimarily whole or simply prepared ingredients.
65–84 · Lightly processedSimple formulations or culinary ingredients with limited processing clues.
40–64 · ProcessedMore processing signals, but no strong evidence of an ultra-processed formulation.
0–39 · Ultra-processed likelyIngredients suggest an industrially formulated food; confirm with the label.

What the score does not measure

Calories, macronutrients, allergens, safety, pesticide residues, microplastics, heavy metals, glycaemic impact, or whether a product is objectively healthy.

How to interpret 100

“100” means no processing clues were detected in the supplied ingredient text. It cannot certify that an item is literally 100% natural.

⚠️ Important: OCR can misread packaging, crowdsourced ingredient lists can be outdated, and NOVA categories are not perfectly determined from ingredients alone. Foods that contain additives are not automatically harmful, and minimally processed foods aren't always nutritionally superior. Always verify allergies directly on the package.
